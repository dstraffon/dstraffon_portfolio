Side projects, built with AI

Sharpening my skills and having fun.

AI has changed what one designer can build. Prototyping, coding and iterating used to be separate handoffs, and now they aren’t.

I see that as a shift in the craft, and I want to stay at the front of it. Each of these projects was a way to test how AI fits into my process, from framing the problem to shipping something people can use.

They show the same UX and product thinking as my client work, applied to real, working software, and they’re how I keep my skills current as the tools change.

Pomodoro Pal

Focus and attention

A Pomodoro timer for desktop and mobile that’s meant to feel calm rather than clinical. A live WebGL shader fills the background and changes with your session: ember tones for focus, teal and violet for breaks. It glows brighter as a session nears its end.

Around the animated dial are features that support focus without crowding it: tasks, streaks and a heatmap, a breathing guide, ambient sound and zen mode. All of it comes to about 20 KB and works offline.

Backseat

The road trip companion app

A phone first game companion that turns time on the road into shared play, with quick modes for riddles, trivia, visual puzzles, bingo, “Would You Rather” and daily games. It’s designed for a group sharing one device, so every activity is simple to start and easy to play together.

Building it was practice in AI assisted design: exploring concepts, prototyping screen flows and iterating on visual directions, with my own product judgment keeping the experience cohesive, usable and responsive.

808 Pocket

One click from a rhythm

A compact drum machine that runs in the browser and makes starting a beat feel immediate and playful. It has a responsive 16 step sequencer, eight synthesized drum voices, tempo and volume controls, and pitch tuning for the kick, snare tone and toms.

I built it as a hands on way to get better with AI tools, using them to explore ideas, iterate on features and design, and take the project from concept to a polished experience on desktop and mobile.

Try it live ↗Figma file ↗

Wobble Tower

A physics game about balance

A stacking game for phone and desktop. You drag smiling blocks onto a small island and build as high as you can before three of them fall into the sea. Your score is the tower’s peak height in meters, and the sky turns from day to dusk to night as you climb.

There are twelve block shapes, from squares and planks to wheels, domes and L shapes, plus a Hold slot to save one for later. You rotate a block with the round buttons, by twisting a second finger on a phone, or with the arrow keys and scroll wheel on a computer. Matter.js handles the physics, and the browser synthesizes every sound, so the game has no audio files.
