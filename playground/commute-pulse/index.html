CommutePulse

I live in New York, and I've always been curious how the rest of the city gets around: when it wakes up, how fast the rush builds, and who's still riding at 3 AM.

So I mapped it. This plays back every train in the MTA's published timetable over one day, and the map's light follows the sun. Each dot is one train, placed by its schedule.

Data. MTA New York City Transit static GTFS feed for the subway and Staten Island Railway (fall 2026 schedule). Borough outlines from NYC Department of City Planning, via dwillis/nyc-maps.

Trains are placed by the timetable, not live tracking. Between two stops a train moves at an even pace along the track geometry; real trains speed up, slow down and run late. Times past midnight in the schedule wrap around to the start of the day. The map's daylight follows sunrise and sunset for late November in New York.

Counts. "Trains on the move" counts scheduled trips between their first and last stop at that minute. Planned work and service changes that the MTA publishes separately aren't included. Transfer complexes that share a name, like Times Sq-42 St, count as one station.
